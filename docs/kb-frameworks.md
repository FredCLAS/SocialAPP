## 1. Growth sequence (all platforms)

The same eight-step arc repeats for Facebook, Instagram, and YouTube:

1. Set up the profile or page so it can be found and understood at a glance.
2. Post on a steady cadence. Consistency matters more than volume.
3. Invite people who already know you.
4. Link the profile from every owned surface (site, email footer, cards).
5. Reply to every comment and engage in the niche daily.
6. Test messages. Promote the posts that get the strongest response, to the target audience.
7. Only after you have a core of engaged followers, introduce the offer.
8. Measure the first sale or conversion and repeat what worked.

App mapping: cadence engine (M2), engagement reporting only (engagement is never automated), boost recommendations at view milestones (boost stays approval-gated).

## 2. Facebook

- Page: complete the profile fields and imagery before posting.
- Post types: image, video, live, questions, polls. Mix them by goal (reach, engagement, sales).
- Pre-post check: audience fit, a useful or engaging point, one clear call to action, a time of day that suits the audience.
- Live: outline talking points, start with a welcome and a hook, end with a call to action, then publish the replay to the feed.
- Planning: a weekly planner tracks planned posts per day, comments answered, and a content mix split (motivational, educational, entertaining, questions) plus a monetization mix (messages, website).

App mapping: content_type mix per platform (M2 config), pre-post checklist as a field check before approval, weekly report for the planner metrics.

## 3. Instagram

- Account: switch to a business account first. The profile is the only place a clickable link sits outside Stories, so the link must lead somewhere useful.
- Content types: feed posts, carousels, Stories, Reels, Live, Highlights (saved Stories).
- Monetization is one of two routes: send people to a website (link from profile or Stories), or start a direct conversation. Pick based on what is sold.
- Captions, hashtags, and alt text are treated as separate levers. Each is written with the audience in mind.

App mapping: Reels (video format), profile link checked against allowed CTA hosts (M2B), alt text as a future field.

## 4. YouTube

- Research first: define the viewer, write a short channel promise, pick primary keyword phrases, choose a posting rhythm, study competitor channels.
- Channel setup: custom banner and profile image, a channel trailer, consistent naming.
- Regular uploads, plus Shorts and live where useful.
- Optimize every video before publishing: title with the keyword phrase, description with links and timestamps, category, captions, thumbnail.
- Promotion: share each video to other social channels with the link in a comment, add the channel link to every owned surface, and use a subscribe link where available.
- Monetization milestones: custom URL after 100 subscribers. Partner Program eligibility at 1,000 subscribers and 4,000 public watch hours (YouTube's published thresholds; confirm current values).

App mapping: webinar replay upload (M4) with chapters and a Calendly CTA in the description, optimization checklist as a pre-publish gate, replay private until approved.

## 5. Sales funnel

Core funnel types in the course: appointment funnel (book a call), product funnel (sell directly), webinar funnel (teach, then offer). Each has the same parts: a lead capture page, follow-up, a booking or checkout page, and a thank-you or upsell page.

Build order:
1. Choose the funnel type from the offer.
2. Use a CRM or email system to store leads. The course advises keeping lead storage outside the funnel builder.
3. Take payments only from a legally set-up business. Route phone or invoice sales through a payment processor that suits that flow.
4. Build pages from templates, then customize.
5. Test every path end to end on the live domain, on desktop and mobile, ideally with a second person running the test.
6. Drive traffic (free first, then paid). Split-test headlines and offers from real data.

Hooks and lead magnets: lead with a specific, concrete headline; the lead magnet must be worth the email address.

App mapping: lead engine (M8) with stages found → qualified → contacted → replied → booked → won/lost, Calendly as the booking page, MailerLite for opt-in (webinar registration), and the approval gate for every outbound message.

## 6. Cross-cutting rules we keep

- Never automate engagement (likes, comments, follows). Report only.
- No promised results. Drafts avoid guarantees (already enforced in M2B).
- Every outbound message goes through an approval record first.
- The course's own rule "do not share or resell" governs what we publish: principles here are our own words, and no course text leaves the private folder.
